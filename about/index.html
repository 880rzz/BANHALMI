Oeuvre · Biography · Curatorial context

I never planned to become a photographer. Then a camera found its way into my hands.

The first camera came into my hands during military service. Since then I have photographed leaders, artists, weddings, international brands and people going through some of the hardest periods of their lives. The settings changed. The question did not: how can a photograph show the person rather than merely record their appearance?

Biography

From discipline to attention

As a child I wanted to become an astronaut. I was drawn to whatever had not yet been understood. I did not end up exploring space, but the same curiosity stayed with me and gradually turned toward people.

My first profession was information technology, and I completed an Information Engineering degree in 2005. Photography developed alongside it. In 2006 I founded the Hungarian Defence Forces’ professional photo studio, in 2015 I completed a programme at the New York Institute of Photography, and in 2018 I qualified as an applied photographer in Budapest.

New York shifted my way of seeing in 2014. I began to treat place, person and image as parts of the same relationship, rather than placing a subject in front of a background. That approach still connects my commissioned portraits, documentary projects, books and curatorial work.

I live and work in Vienna, with Budapest remaining an active professional base. Alongside commissions and exhibitions, I teach, build photography communities and develop projects that use visual culture for education and practical community benefit.

Curatorial frame

There is always a person behind the picture.

My long-term projects have dealt with ageing, illness, historical wounds, women’s stories, fatherhood, the body and the need for touch. These subjects ask for care, patience and honesty. That is why I return to them.

I do not want to turn the person in front of me into a concept. I give them time, clear boundaries and a space in which they do not need to perform. The discipline I learned in the military is still part of the process. Its purpose is not distance, but structure and dignity.

That balance between structure and emotion also shapes my executive work. A leadership portrait and a fine-art body study serve different purposes, but both rely on trust, attention, clear boundaries and the ability to recognise presence without forcing a performance.

What the road taught me

Some turns change more than the route.

A serious accident forced me to stop and ask what I wanted photography to be for. I decided that a picture should do more than look good. It should carry meaning for the person who trusted me to make it. That decision still guides both portraits and long-term projects.

New York taught me the next lesson. I arrived with very little money and a large idea. People helped, doors opened and the project found its way forward. Since then I have trusted careful preparation, human connection and the courage to begin before every answer is available.

Recognition followed, including the OM SYSTEM Ambassador role. I value it. But the projects that stay closest to me are the ones that became useful beyond the photograph: the oncology exhibition created with almost a hundred contributors, the community portraits that helped a school, and the workshops where knowledge keeps moving from one person to another.

That is also how I think about a commission for a leader or an organisation. The photograph matters. What it helps build afterwards matters even more. Read the longer interview ↗ · Read the Vienna portrait ↗

Professional practice

Commissioned practice and professional context

Alongside the independent oeuvre, I have worked across executive portraiture, editorial and brand photography, international sporting and cultural events, campaigns and institutional communication. The professional context includes assignments or collaborations connected with Coca-Cola, Samsung, Olympus / OM System, the Dutch national football team, international conferences and a wide range of private and corporate clients.

Today the work is based between Vienna and Budapest and can expand through selected photographers, retouchers and coordinators when the scale of a project requires it. Visual authorship and final responsibility remain centralized.

Featured work · documented international reach

The Péter Magyar portrait: from EUFÓRIA to international editorial reuse

I photographed Péter Magyar at Heroes’ Square in Budapest on 15 March 2026 at 16:57 as the first work in EUFÓRIA – The Anatomy of Presence, an artistic project conceived as a contemporary homage to Robert Capa’s principle of proximity. Wikimedia Commons records the creator, depicted person, date, location, project relationship and CC BY-SA 4.0 licence.

The open licence allowed the portrait to move independently through encyclopaedic, educational and editorial contexts. The documented uses below include publications from different countries and sharply different political viewpoints. Their inclusion records the photograph’s reuse and attribution only; it does not endorse the articles’ political framing.
