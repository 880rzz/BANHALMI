Brand photography · Visual positioning strategy

The photographs your company gets recognised by.

A company has a face too. You see it in its leaders, its team, the atmosphere of the workplace and the small details people inside may no longer notice. Those are the pieces I bring together.

I do not want to hand you a folder of beautiful photographs that no one knows how to use. I create a practical image library for your website, press, recruitment, presentations and campaigns.

What the partnership includes

One shoot is only useful when the images work everywhere they need to.

Before the camera comes out, we define who the images are for, where they will appear and what they must communicate. The result is not simply a folder of photographs, but a usable visual system for leadership, brand and communication teams.

1. Visual brief

Purpose, audience, tone, locations and practical constraints are agreed before production.

2. Use map

LinkedIn, press, website, annual report, employer branding and event communication are planned together.

3. Preparation

Wardrobe, location, light, schedule and the people involved are prepared so the day stays focused.

4. Direction on set

Clear guidance without forcing a pose, with room for the moment when the person becomes present.

5. Structured selection

The final set is chosen by role and use, not only by which frame looks strongest in isolation.

6. Consistent delivery

Files are prepared for the agreed channels so later publications and future shoots remain coherent.

Strategic Visual Partnership

I learn about the company first. The photographs come next.

Before planning a shot list, I spend time understanding how the company actually works: who carries its authority, where its culture becomes visible and which situations matter to customers, candidates and partners. The photography day should begin with decisions already made, not with guesses.

1 · Understand

We begin with the organisation, its goals, culture, audiences and the business situations in which the visual presence must work.

2 · Position

We define the leadership presence and visual principles that should communicate trust, credibility and professionalism.

3 · Create

Executive portraits, brand photography, employer-branding imagery and event documentation are planned to work together.

4 · Roll out

The image system is adapted for LinkedIn, websites, press, presentations, recruitment, campaigns and international communication.

5 · Evolve

The partnership continues as leadership, teams and communication needs change, keeping the visual language consistent over time.

From positioning to a practical image library

I begin with identity, audience, market context, differentiation and the current gap between how the brand wants to be understood and how it is presently seen. Together we define the visual territory: level of formality, emotional distance, authority, accessibility, environment, colour, light and recurring motifs.

The photography then translates this positioning into a usable structured image set. Founder portraits, team situations, workplace details, products, editorial scenes and campaign images serve different channels, but communicate the same strategic position.

Process

How visual positioning becomes photography

01

Strategic context

We clarify audience, market, offer, differentiators, desired perception and the business situations in which the images must work.

02

Visual positioning

I define the visual principles: authority or accessibility, distance or intimacy, environments, wardrobe, light, colour, framing and narrative rhythm.

03

Production

The shoot follows a deliberate shot plan while leaving room for real interaction, personality and unplanned moments.

04

Image system and rollout

The final set is structured for websites, press, LinkedIn, presentations, campaigns, employer branding and long-term content use.

Delivery system

What arrives, when it arrives and how it can be used

The accepted written offer records the preview deadline, the client-selection deadline and the final-delivery deadline. This keeps timing visible before production begins instead of leaving it to assumptions.

Selection and approval

A structured proof gallery is delivered for selection where the project requires client choice. The number of final images, approval rounds and decision-makers are fixed in the written scope.

Final image package

Approved photographs are supplied as high-resolution master files and web-ready versions. Agreed crops for LinkedIn, press, website, presentations or campaigns are included only when listed in the offer.

Retouching and file structure

Retouching follows the agreed visual standard. Files use a clear naming structure, and delivery notes identify master files, web files and platform-specific variants.

Timing and follow-up

Preview, selection and final-delivery dates are project-specific and written into the accepted offer. Urgent or same-day delivery is available only when agreed before production.

File governance

Storage, access and deletion

The written offer records the delivery route, access period and any project-specific retention requirement. Delivery is not a promise of permanent archive storage.

Controlled access

Preview galleries, download links and working folders are limited to the agreed project team. Access links may expire and should not be forwarded outside the authorised group.

Delivered files and working files

The client receives the agreed final files. RAW captures, rejected frames, contact sheets and intermediate retouching files remain working material unless the written offer expressly includes them.

Retention and backup

BANHALMI may keep project files for a reasonable operational period, but does not guarantee permanent storage. The client is responsible for securely backing up the delivered package after download.

Deletion requests

A deletion request can be submitted in writing. Files are removed where the request applies, except where continued retention is required for an accepted contract, accounting, legal claims or another valid legal basis.

Publication control

Image use, approvals and reference rights

The written offer defines the intended channels, decision-makers and any restrictions before production. Delivery of files does not by itself authorise BANHALMI to publish a project as a reference.

Agreed use

The client may use the delivered images within the licence recorded in the offer, including the agreed internal, press, employer-branding, social or campaign channels. New uses outside that scope should be cleared in writing.

People and permissions

The client identifies who may be photographed and is responsible for the organisational notices, participant permissions and internal approvals required for the planned use. Sensitive or restricted participants can be excluded before or during production.

Approval before release

Where approval is required, only the selected and approved final images are released for publication. Contact sheets, previews and unapproved variants are not publication files.

Portfolio and later restrictions

BANHALMI uses client work in its portfolio, awards, press or educational material only where that use is agreed or otherwise lawfully permitted. A later restriction or removal request can be submitted in writing and will be assessed against existing licences, publications and legal obligations.

Project governance

Approval, confidentiality and publication

The written offer identifies who may approve the work, what remains private and where the finished images may be published.

One authorised approver

The commissioning organisation names one authorised approver. Projects with several stakeholders return one single consolidated feedback round rather than conflicting individual instructions.

Confidential by default

Unreleased portraits, internal events, strategy material, contact sheets and project discussions remain confidential by default. Access is limited to the agreed project team.

Publication requires permission

BANHALMI does not publish client images as a portfolio, case study, press item or social post without written permission. The approved channels and timing are recorded separately from the usage licence.

Internal use and third parties

Internal distribution within the commissioning organisation follows the accepted offer. Transfer to agencies, media partners or other third parties must fit the agreed licence or receive written approval.

Scheduling and continuity

Booking, rescheduling and contingency

The written offer confirms when a date becomes reserved, how changes are handled and which costs can arise if circumstances move after production has started.

Confirmed reservation

A date is reserved only after written acceptance and any agreed booking payment. An enquiry, calculator result or calendar conversation alone does not hold a production date.

Rescheduling and delay

Changes caused by executive availability, venue access, weather or programme timing are handled in writing. We first seek a proportionate replacement date; confirmed external costs remain visible.

Cancellation and external costs

Cancellation terms are stated in the individual offer. Work already completed and documented third-party costs may remain payable; no undefined penalty is added outside the accepted agreement.

Illness, failure and force majeure

If illness, essential equipment failure or an event beyond reasonable control prevents delivery, BANHALMI prioritises a replacement date, equivalent professional solution or fair adjustment of the affected scope.
